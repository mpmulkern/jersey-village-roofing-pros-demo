Roof & Attic Ventilation in Jersey Village, TX

Attic ventilation is the least visible part of a roof and one of the most consequential. In the Houston climate an under-ventilated attic runs extreme temperatures through the long summer and holds humidity year round, and both shorten the life of the roof above it. Jersey Village Roofing Pros designs and installs balanced ventilation on Jersey Village and Houston-area homes, usually as part of a replacement but often as a standalone correction.

Quick Answer: how much ventilation does an attic need?

One square foot of net free vent area per 150 square feet of attic floor, or per 300 square feet with a vapour barrier and balanced airflow. Split it roughly half and half: low intake at the soffits, high exhaust at the ridge. Exhaust without matching intake does very little.

Ventilation Components We Install

Ridge Vents

Continuous exhaust along the peak, cut into the decking and capped with ridge shingles. The most effective exhaust for most pitched Houston roofs, with nothing mechanical to fail.

Soffit Intake

The half that is usually missing. Vented soffit panels or continuous strip intake feed cool air in at the eaves, and we clear insulation blocking the airflow path.

Static & Turbine Vents

Box and turbine exhaust for rooflines where a continuous ridge run is not practical — hips, short ridges, and complex cut-ups.

Baffles & Insulation Correction

Rafter baffles keep the intake channel open where blown insulation has drifted over the eaves and choked it off.

Why It Matters More on the Gulf Coast

Heat is the obvious pressure. A poorly vented Houston attic can run far above outdoor temperature through the afternoon, and that heat loads the underside of the decking, cooking the shingles from below while the sun works on them from above. The visible result is premature granule loss, curling, and a roof that reaches the end of its life years earlier than its warranty suggested.

Humidity is the quieter half, and the one most homeowners never consider. Gulf Coast air carries moisture into the attic continuously, and without airflow it condenses on the coolest surfaces available: nail tips, decking, rafters. That is the origin of the rusted fasteners, dark staining, and soft sheathing we find during tear-off on houses whose owners believed the roof was simply old. Ventilation is also cheap relative to what it protects — correcting intake and exhaust costs a fraction of the replacement it postpones.

  • Net free vent area calculated against actual attic square footage
  • Intake and exhaust balanced, not exhaust piled on alone
  • Soffit intake cleared and baffled where insulation has blocked it
  • Ridge vent cut and installed to manufacturer slot width
  • Bathroom and dryer ducts confirmed venting outside, not into the attic
  • Works as a standalone job or as part of a full roof replacement

The Mistake We Correct Most Often

Adding exhaust without adding intake. A homeowner notices a hot attic, has turbines or a powered fan fitted, and the problem does not improve. Air leaving at the ridge has to be replaced from somewhere, and if the soffits are blocked or absent, the easiest source is the conditioned house below, pulled up through gaps around light fittings and the attic hatch. The fan then runs steadily, exporting air you have paid to cool.

Mixing exhaust types on the same attic space causes a related problem: a ridge vent and powered fans together can short-circuit, with the fan drawing air straight back down through the ridge rather than along the deck from the eaves. We assess the whole attic as one system before specifying anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much attic ventilation does a Houston home need?

The standard rule is one square foot of net free vent area per 150 square feet of attic floor, or per 300 square feet where a vapour barrier is present and intake and exhaust are balanced. Roughly half should be low intake at the soffits and half high exhaust at or near the ridge.

Can bad attic ventilation shorten the life of my roof?

Yes. Trapped heat bakes shingles from underneath, which in the Houston climate can cost several years of service life and accelerates granule loss and curling. Trapped humidity is the second half of the problem, condensing on decking and rafters and causing the mould and soft sheathing we frequently find during tear-off.

Are ridge vents better than powered attic fans?

For most Houston-area homes, yes. A continuous ridge vent paired with adequate soffit intake moves air along the whole underside of the deck with no motor to fail and no electricity used. A powered fan with insufficient intake can actively pull conditioned air out of the living space through ceiling gaps, raising cooling costs rather than lowering them.

How do I know if my attic is poorly ventilated?

Common signs are an attic that is punishingly hot late in the day, upstairs rooms that will not hold temperature, rusted nail tips or dark staining on the underside of the decking, and damp sheathing after humid spells. A roof inspection checks net free vent area and whether the soffit intake is blocked by insulation.
