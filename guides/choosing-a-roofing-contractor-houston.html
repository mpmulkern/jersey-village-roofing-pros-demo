How to Choose a Roofing Contractor in Houston, TX

A roof is one of the largest single purchases a homeowner makes, and in Texas it is bought in a market with no state licensing. That combination is why the weeks after a Houston hail event bring a wave of unfamiliar trucks and doorstep offers. This guide covers what can actually be verified, what the common tactics look like, and which questions separate a contractor who will still exist in five years from one who will not.

Quick Answer: the three checks that matter most

Get the general liability and workers' compensation certificates from the insurer, not the contractor. Confirm a real local address with trading history behind it. Get the full scope in writing before any money moves. Almost everything that goes wrong in this trade is caught by one of those three.

Why There Is No Texas Roofing Licence

Texas does not license roofing contractors at state level. There is no state exam, no registration number to quote, and no licensing board with the power to revoke anything. Voluntary programmes exist, along with manufacturer certifications that mean a crew has been trained on a specific product line, but none of these is a state licence and some contractors blur that distinction deliberately.

The practical consequence is that anyone can print business cards and start roofing houses in Houston tomorrow. It also explains the migratory pattern after severe weather: crews follow hail across states precisely because there is no local credential to lose. That is not an argument against out-of-town companies as a class, but it is an argument for verifying who you are dealing with rather than assuming a gatekeeper has done it for you.

Verify Insurance Properly

Two policies matter. General liability covers damage the crew causes to your property. Workers' compensation covers injury to a worker on your roof, and its absence is the one that can reach your own homeowner policy if someone is hurt on your property. Texas does not require private employers to carry workers' compensation, so its presence is a meaningful signal rather than a formality.

Ask for certificates issued directly by the insurer or agent, with your name as certificate holder, rather than a photocopy handed over on the driveway. Check the policy dates cover your scheduled work, not just the day you asked. A contractor who treats this request as routine is telling you they answer it often; one who becomes evasive has told you everything you need.

  • General liability certificate, current dates, from the insurer
  • Workers' compensation certificate, or a clear statement it is not carried
  • Physical local address that resolves to a real place of business
  • Trading history in the Houston metro, not just a new registration
  • Written references from jobs completed more than a year ago
  • Manufacturer certifications named specifically, not implied

The Deductible Offer, and Why It Is a Crime

The most common post-storm pitch in Houston is some version of "we will cover your deductible" or "we will make it disappear in the paperwork". In Texas it is illegal for a contractor to pay, waive, rebate, or absorb a homeowner's insurance deductible, and a homeowner who knowingly accepts the arrangement is a party to insurance fraud, not a victim of it.

Beyond the legal exposure, the economics tell you what is happening. The money has to come from somewhere: either the scope submitted to your insurer is inflated to cover the gap, or the work is quietly reduced to fit. Either way, the roof you receive is not the roof the claim paid for. A contractor who opens the relationship with an illegal offer has given you a clear preview of everything that follows.

What a Proper Written Estimate Contains

A real estimate is a specification, not a number on a business card. It should name the shingle manufacturer, product line, and colour; state the underlayment type; specify whether decking replacement is included and at what rate per sheet if more is found during tear-off; list flashing, valley, vent, and drip edge work; address ventilation; and set out clean-up, magnetic nail sweep, and debris removal. It should also state the workmanship warranty term separately from the manufacturer's material warranty, because those are different things and are routinely conflated in sales conversations.

Get three of these. Three quotes reveal the genuine price band for your specific roof and expose both the outlier too cheap to be complete and the one padded above market. Compare the scopes line by line rather than the totals, because the cheapest number is very often the one omitting decking allowance, proper underlayment, or ventilation correction that the others include.

Warning Signs Worth Walking Away From

Several patterns recur often enough in the Houston market to treat as decisive. A demand for full payment up front, particularly in cash, is the clearest. High-pressure timing ("this price is only good today") is the second, since no legitimate roofing quote expires within hours. Vagueness about the company's physical address, or a contact route that is only a mobile number, is the third.

Also be cautious about signing anything described as merely authorising an inspection. In some cases those documents are contingency contracts that bind you to the contractor if the insurer approves any claim at all. Read what is in front of you, and be wary of any pitch that depends on you not doing so.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do roofing contractors need a licence in Texas?

No. Texas does not license roofing contractors at state level, so there is no state credential to check and no licensing board to revoke one. Verification falls entirely to the homeowner, which means checking insurance certificates, local trading history, and written references instead.

What insurance should a Houston roofer carry?

Two policies: general liability, which covers damage to your property, and workers' compensation, which covers injury to crew on your roof. Ask for the certificates directly from the insurer or agent rather than a photocopy, and check the dates are current for your scheduled work.

Is it legal for a roofer to waive my insurance deductible?

No. In Texas it is illegal for a contractor to pay, waive, rebate, or absorb a homeowner's insurance deductible, and a homeowner who knowingly accepts the arrangement is party to insurance fraud. A contractor offering it is signalling both dishonesty and a scope that will be padded to cover the gap.

How many roofing quotes should I get in Houston?

Three is the practical number. It reveals the realistic price band for your roof and exposes both the outlier that is too cheap to be complete and the one padded well above market. Compare the written scopes line by line, not just the totals, because the cheapest quote is often the one leaving out decking, underlayment, or ventilation.

Should I pay a roofing deposit up front?

A modest deposit to secure materials and a schedule slot is normal practice. Paying for the whole job before work begins is not, and a demand for full payment up front, especially in cash, is the single clearest warning sign in the trade.
