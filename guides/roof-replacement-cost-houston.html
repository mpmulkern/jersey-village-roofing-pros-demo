How Much Does a Roof Replacement Cost in Houston?

It is the first question almost every homeowner asks, and most published answers are useless because they average the entire country. Houston has its own labour market, its own storm-driven demand cycles, and its own building stock. This guide gives real 2026 ranges for the Jersey Village and greater Houston area, and explains what actually moves the number.

Quick Answer

Most Houston-area homeowners pay roughly $9,000 to $18,000 for a full architectural asphalt shingle roof replacement in 2026. A typical 2,000 sq ft single-storey home lands near the middle. Metal roofing commonly runs $25,000 to $45,000 for the same house. If a covered storm event caused the damage, your out-of-pocket cost is usually just your deductible.

Cost by Roofing Material

Material is the single largest line item, and the spread is wide. These are installed prices per roofing square (100 square feet) for the Houston metro in 2026, including tear-off and standard underlayment.

3-Tab Asphalt

$350–$450 per square. The builder-grade option. Shortest life in Texas heat, typically 12 to 15 years, and increasingly hard to justify against architectural.

Architectural Asphalt

$450–$700 per square. The default choice for most Houston homes. Better wind rating and 20 to 25 year realistic service life.

Impact-Resistant Shingles

$600–$900 per square. Class 4 rated. Costs more up front but many Texas carriers discount premiums, which can recover the difference over time.

Standing Seam Metal

$1,200–$2,000 per square. Two to three times the asphalt cost, two to three times the lifespan. See our metal roofing page.

Cost by Roof Size

Roof area is not the same as house floor area — pitch, overhangs, and multiple storeys all change the relationship. As a rough guide for architectural asphalt in the Houston metro:

  • 1,200 sq ft home (about 15 squares): $7,000–$11,000
  • 2,000 sq ft home (about 22 squares): $10,000–$15,500
  • 2,800 sq ft home (about 30 squares): $13,500–$21,000
  • 3,500 sq ft home (about 38 squares): $17,000–$27,000

What Drives the Price Up

Two identical-looking houses on the same street can quote thousands apart, and it is rarely the contractor being greedy. The main factors:

  • Decking condition. Nobody knows what is under the shingles until tear-off. Rotted sheathing runs $70–$120 per sheet to replace, and a roof that has leaked quietly for years can need a dozen sheets.
  • Pitch. Anything steeper than 6:12 needs fall protection and slows the crew down, adding 10–20 percent to labour.
  • Complexity. Valleys, dormers, skylights, chimneys, and multiple roof planes all add cut waste and flashing labour. A simple gable roof is the cheapest shape to replace.
  • Layers to remove. A second existing layer roughly doubles tear-off and disposal cost.
  • Ventilation upgrades. Many older Houston roofs are under-ventilated. Correcting it during replacement costs a few hundred dollars and materially extends the new roof's life.
  • Storm-season demand. After a major hail event, metro-wide demand spikes and prices firm up. Booking outside the post-storm rush is genuinely cheaper.

How Insurance Changes the Maths

If a covered event caused the damage — hail or wind, not age — the relevant number stops being the total and becomes your deductible. Texas wind and hail deductibles are frequently percentage-based, typically 1 to 2 percent of the dwelling coverage, so on a $400,000 policy that is $4,000 to $8,000 rather than a flat $1,000.

The other detail that catches people out is the roof endorsement. A replacement cost value policy pays what it costs to replace the roof today. An actual cash value policy pays the depreciated value of a roof that was already fifteen years old, and the gap is yours to fund. Check which one you have before you need it. Getting a Quote Worth Comparing

A quote you can actually compare has to be itemised: material and grade, tear-off and disposal, underlayment type, flashing and vent replacement, a stated per-sheet decking rate, and the workmanship warranty term. If a contractor will not put a decking rate in writing, that is where the surprise will come from. Be wary of any door-knocker after a storm who wants a signature the same day, and of quotes that arrive without anyone having been on the roof.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a roof replacement cost in Houston in 2026?

Most Houston-area homeowners pay roughly $9,000 to $18,000 for a full architectural asphalt shingle roof replacement in 2026, with a typical 2,000 square foot single-storey home landing near the middle of that range. Metal roofing runs materially higher, commonly $25,000 to $45,000 for the same house.

Why do roof replacement quotes vary so much for the same house?

The three biggest swing factors are decking condition that is only visible after tear-off, roof complexity such as pitch and the number of valleys and penetrations, and shingle grade. A quote that omits decking replacement or ventilation upgrades will look cheaper until the work starts.

Will insurance pay for my roof replacement in Texas?

Insurance covers roof replacement when the failure comes from a covered event such as hail or wind, not from age or wear. If your claim is approved, you generally pay only your deductible, though policies with actual cash value roof endorsements pay depreciated value rather than full replacement cost.

How long does a roof replacement take?

A typical Houston-area single-family home is torn off and re-roofed in one to two days for asphalt shingles, or two to four days for metal. Weather delays are the main variable during Gulf Coast storm season.

Is it cheaper to repair or replace a roof?

Repair is cheaper when damage is localised and the roof has years of service life left. Replacement is usually the better value once a roof is past about 15 years, has damage across multiple slopes, or has needed repeated repairs, because each repair on an ageing roof buys progressively less time.
