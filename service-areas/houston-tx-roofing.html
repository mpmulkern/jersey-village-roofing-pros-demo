What Houston Does to Roofs

Four regional pressures show up on nearly every inspection here. Heat is constant: long summers drive attic temperatures hard and cook shingles from beneath, which is why a Houston asphalt roof commonly reaches the end of its life closer to 15 years than the 25 or 30 printed on the wrapper. Humidity compounds it, condensing in under-ventilated attics and rotting decking from the inside while the surface still looks serviceable.

Then there is water volume. Houston rainfall arrives in intense bursts that overwhelm undersized or clogged gutters, and water that cannot leave the roofline finds fascia, soffit, and eventually the wall assembly. Finally, wind and hail: the metro sits far enough inland to escape the worst surge but squarely in the path of both spring hail tracks and the outer bands of tropical systems, and edge uplift on a nailed shingle field is a recurring finding after every significant blow.

Choosing a Roofer in an Unlicensed State

Texas does not license roofing contractors at state level. That surprises most homeowners, and it is exactly why Houston attracts out-of-town crews in the weeks after a hail event — there is no licence to lose. The practical protections are ordinary but effective: ask for current certificates of general liability and workers' compensation insurance rather than a verbal assurance, confirm a real local address with a history behind it, and get the full scope in writing before any money changes hands.

Be particularly wary of the post-storm doorstep pitch that offers to absorb your deductible. In Texas that arrangement is insurance fraud, and the homeowner who agrees to it is a party to it. A contractor willing to start the relationship that way is telling you something useful about how the rest of the job will go.
